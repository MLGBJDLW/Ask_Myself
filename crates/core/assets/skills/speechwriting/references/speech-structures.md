# Speech Structures

## Audience Map
- Belief now: what the audience likely thinks before the talk.
- Desired belief: what should change.
- Resistance: what objection, fear, fatigue, or incentive blocks change.
- Proof burden: what evidence or story is needed to earn trust.
- Action path: what the audience can do next.

## Useful Arcs
- Problem-solution: pain, cause, failed attempts, new approach, proof, call to action.
- Story-proof-ask: personal story, general lesson, evidence, audience application, ask.
- Monroe sequence: attention, need, satisfaction, visualization, action.
- Before-after-bridge: current world, better world, bridge to get there.
- Tension-release: expectation, contradiction, deeper truth, practical implication.
- Demonstration: promise, show the thing, explain the mechanism, prove repeatability, invite use.

## Formats
- 3-minute talk: one story, one idea, one ask.
- 10-minute talk: hook, stakes, three moves, close.
- 20-minute keynote: opening story, central tension, three proof blocks, emotional turn, charge.
- Investor pitch: market pain, product insight, traction, business model, team, ask.
- Academic talk: question, gap, method, finding, limitation, implication.
- Town hall: acknowledge reality, explain decision, name tradeoffs, show path, invite trust.

## Rhetorical Tools
- Triad: three parallel items for rhythm.
- Callback: return to an opening image or line at the end.
- Contrast: not X, but Y.
- Ladder: individual story -> team/org meaning -> broader principle.
- Compression: turn a complex point into a repeatable phrase.
- Silence: use pauses after stakes, jokes, grief, or the ask.

## Rehearsal Notes
- Mark breaths before rehearsing, not after.
- Read aloud once for rhythm, once for clarity, once for time.
- Cut anything that only works visually on the page.
- If the speaker trips twice on a sentence, rewrite the sentence.

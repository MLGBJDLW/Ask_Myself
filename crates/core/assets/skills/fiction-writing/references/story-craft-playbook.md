# Story Craft Playbook

## Outline Depths
- Premise: one sentence with protagonist, desire, obstacle, stakes, and irony.
- Logline: premise plus hook, genre promise, and central dramatic question.
- Synopsis: beginning, middle, end, and transformation in 3-8 paragraphs.
- Beat sheet: 12-40 causal turns, each changing pressure or information.
- Chapter outline: chapter goal, POV, setting, conflict, reveal, emotional turn, hook.
- Scene card: POV, time/place, want, obstacle, tactic, turn, cost, next pressure.
- Continuity bible: canon facts, timeline, character state, relationships, unresolved promises.

## Structure Lenses
Use a structure lens only when it clarifies a current problem.

### Three-Act
Best for mainstream novels, film-like pacing, commercial arcs.
- Act 1: setup, inciting incident, refusal/complication, first commitment.
- Act 2A: trials, new tactics, midpoint revelation or false victory.
- Act 2B: pressure rises, losses compound, protagonist's old strategy fails.
- Act 3: final plan, climax choice, cost, resolution.

### Seven-Point
Best for diagnosing causal movement.
- Hook: status quo and character deficit.
- Plot turn 1: external pressure forces action.
- Pinch 1: antagonist/system shows real threat.
- Midpoint: protagonist shifts from reactive to active.
- Pinch 2: cost becomes personal.
- Plot turn 2: final clue, loss, or self-knowledge unlocks the ending.
- Resolution: answer the dramatic question.

### Save-the-Cat Style Beat Sheet
Best for commercial pacing checks.
- Opening image, theme stated, setup, catalyst, debate, break into two.
- B-story, fun and games, midpoint, bad guys close in, all is lost.
- Dark night, break into three, finale, final image.

### Hero's Journey
Best for mythic, adventure, fantasy, quest, and identity arcs.
- Ordinary world, call, refusal, mentor, threshold, tests/allies/enemies.
- Approach, ordeal, reward, road back, resurrection, return with change.

### Kishotenketsu
Best for low-conflict, slice-of-life, literary, puzzle, or contrast-driven stories.
- Ki: establish world and pattern.
- Sho: develop pattern with variation.
- Ten: turn/recontextualization.
- Ketsu: reconciliation or synthesis.

## Genre Architecture
- Mystery: crime/question, suspect web, clue ladder, red herrings, midpoint reframe, solution that makes prior clues inevitable.
- Thriller: visible clock, escalating threat, pursuit/reversal, narrowing options, moral cost, final confrontation.
- Romance: meet/collision, attraction with incompatibility, false intimacy, barrier exposed, breakup/dark moment, earned choice.
- Fantasy: wonder promise, rule discovery, faction pressure, magic cost, worldview expansion, climactic use of learned rule.
- Science fiction: speculative premise, implication chain, social/technical constraint, unintended consequence, idea-driven climax.
- Horror: normal rupture, rule discovery, dread escalation, isolation, transgression or revelation, survival/cost.
- Coming-of-age: lack/need, boundary crossing, disillusionment, self-definition, irreversible maturity cost.
- Web serial: quick hook, clear desire, frequent micro-payoffs, cliffhangers, visible progression, recurring cast pressure.

## Character Arc Checks
- Want: what the character actively pursues.
- Need: what must change internally.
- Wound/belief: the false rule shaping choices.
- Mask: behavior used to survive.
- Pressure test: a scene that makes the old strategy fail.
- Choice: a costly decision proving change or tragic refusal.

## Scene Diagnostics
Use this checklist when a scene feels flat:
1. Can the POV character's immediate want be stated in one sentence?
2. Is there an obstacle with agency or force?
3. Does the tactic change at least once?
4. Is there a turn the reader can feel?
5. Does the ending create a consequence, question, or new pressure?
6. Could the scene be removed without changing plot, relationship, or theme? If yes, merge or cut it.

## Revision Passes
- Macro pass: premise, dramatic question, causal chain, stakes, ending.
- Arc pass: protagonist want/need, antagonist pressure, relationship movement.
- Scene pass: goal, conflict, turn, hook, pacing.
- Continuity pass: timeline, names, rules, wounds, promises, revealed information.
- Prose pass: POV filter, concrete nouns/verbs, rhythm, dialogue subtext, paragraph shape.

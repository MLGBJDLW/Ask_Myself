# Research Synthesis

## Core Rule
Separate what is sourced, inferred, uncertain, and missing. Prefer primary and authoritative sources; do not smooth over contradictions.

## Workflow
1. Frame the question: decision, audience, scope, time sensitivity, and required confidence.
2. Decompose into 2-5 sub-questions when the request is broad.
3. Gather evidence from the best available source tier:
   - User knowledge base or named files for user's own materials.
   - Primary/official/current sources for changing external facts.
   - Secondary sources only for context, interpretation, or discovery.
4. Build a claim table before synthesis:
   - Claim
   - Source(s)
   - Evidence strength
   - Caveat or contradiction
5. Answer with findings first, then evidence, then uncertainty and next checks.

## Source Triage
- Primary: original docs, official releases, filings, standards, papers, datasets.
- Strong secondary: reputable analysis that cites primary sources.
- Weak secondary: unsourced summaries, forums, marketing pages, AI-generated pages.
- Time-sensitive claims require current verification.

## Output Patterns
- Quick answer: conclusion, evidence bullets, confidence.
- Comparison: criteria table, then recommendation and tradeoffs.
- Literature/source review: search scope, inclusion criteria, themes, disagreements, gaps.
- Due diligence: claim register, risk register, open questions.
- Executive brief: bottom line, key facts, implications, recommended next action.

## Confidence Labels
- High: multiple independent primary/authoritative sources agree.
- Medium: limited sources, strong but not complete evidence, or one authoritative source.
- Low: indirect evidence, mixed sources, or material assumptions.
- No evidence: say what was searched or inspected and what was not found.
